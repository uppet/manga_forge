# Narrative bible

## Premise

The city of Palimpsest survives by surrendering painful memories to the Grand
Archive. Its Editors claim that forgetting prevents war. In truth, discarded
memories remain alive, acquire porcelain masks, and wander the Archive as hostile
redactions. The city is peaceful because the people most harmed by it have been
cut out of history.

Nara, the last Ink Warden, wakes below the Archive with no memory of the night it
fell. Her only guide is Morrow, a sentient calligraphy blade. She enters the living
manuscript to find her younger sister Iori and gradually learns that Morrow was the
First Author—the creator who tore away his own obsession with a perfect world.
That discarded obsession became the final antagonist.

## Principal cast

- **Nara:** An adult young woman and the last Ink Warden. She is practical,
  angry, and deeply afraid that recovering the truth will make her resemble
  the people she fights. Her arc is accepting that an imperfect memory still
  belongs to the person who lived it. Every future visual and voice brief must
  preserve her female identity without sexualizing or weakening her.
- **Iori:** An apprentice archivist able to hear the people inside redacted pages.
  Her discovery of the Archive's original draft caused the Editors to erase her.
- **Morrow / Marginalia:** The remorseful half of the First Author, living as
  Nara's blade. He offers knowledge while concealing his responsibility.
- **The Red Editor:** Act I boss. Believes individual suffering is an acceptable
  price for social stability.
- **The Binder:** Act II boss. Imprisons Iori and physically binds contradictory
  histories into one official account.
- **The First Author:** Act III boss. Morrow's discarded perfectionism. Offers a
  painless, immaculate revision in which nobody retains enough selfhood to hurt.

## Three-act run

### Act I — The Public Archive, Pages 1–4

Nara treats the masks as monsters until the Red Editor's broken mask releases the
ordinary memory of a baker. Her objective changes from finding pages to restoring
people. Enemy language is pursuit, panic, and obvious censorship.

The Public Archive branches through the Whisper Stacks, where erased voices still
answer; the Razor Gallery, whose official portraits attack anyone outside their
frames; or the Black Index, a forbidden catalogue that trades safety for recovered
names.

### Act II — The Forbidden Bindery, Pages 5–8

The arena becomes circular and chain-like. Split memories, name thieves, and
ranged scribes force more deliberate builds. The Binder reveals Iori and forces
Morrow to admit he wrote the world's first draft.

The Bindery branches through the Chain Vault of forcibly reconciled histories, the
Errata Canals where unfinished sentences hunt in wet ink, or Contraband Hall where
memories are bought back from the institution that stole them.

### Act III — The First Press, Pages 9–12

Industrial press shapes replace archive shelves. Wardens and dense bullet patterns
test completed builds. Morrow identifies the First Author as the perfection he cut
out of himself. Nara rejects the premise that safety requires erasure.

The First Press branches through the White Room that tries to erase every wound,
the Red Press that stamps verdicts as physical law, or Loose Leaves—a storm of
unbound testimony no single author can control.

## Endings

- **Margins / Preserve the Scars:** Nara restores every memory with its
  contradictions intact. The Archive becomes public and Morrow becomes an ordinary
  sword. Society survives, but must reckon with what it did.
- **Loose Leaves / Break the Binding:** Nara destroys the authority of the Archive.
  History becomes decentralized testimony. The old order ends; the new world is
  difficult, plural, and impossible for one author to erase.

Both endings unlock continued drafts. Repeated runs represent Nara and Iori
recovering distant shelves; permanent Memory upgrades are fragments the city has
chosen to keep. The codex records restored enemy identities, while escalating
difficulty drafts reinterpret the same journey rather than declaring one ending
non-canonical.

Each repeated draft also charts a different route through the three acts. All 27
route chains are parallel shelves of the same living Archive, making repetition a
canonical recovery of places and witnesses omitted from the first journey.

## Presentation contract

Story is told through seven runtime sequences using seven unique project-bound 2D
manga paintings. Each dialogue beat defines its own focal point, push/pull/drift
motion, emotional accent, and independently animated close-up panel; ink wipes and
brief paper flashes separate beats. Typewriter dialogue, device-aware advance
prompts, and the two-choice ending remain code-driven. Text is kept out of bitmap
art for localization. Combat pauses during story sequences; all sequences remain
skippable one panel at a time with keyboard, mouse, or gamepad.
